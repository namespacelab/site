Why your business should move from SaaS to GaaS

Short answer: SaaS gives your team software to operate. GaaS gives you AI that does part of the work for you, inside that software, with people checking the results. Moving from SaaS to GaaS doesn't mean cancelling your tools. It means handing the repeat work to AI, one job at a time, so your team spends its time on decisions instead of clicks.

What is GaaS?

GaaS is a newer term with two common readings. We use it to mean Generative AI as a Service: a provider builds, runs and looks after AI that drafts, answers and completes tasks for your business. In much of the current industry talk, GaaS also means Agentic AI as a Service, AI agents that carry out multi-step work. The term gained attention around NVIDIA's GTC 2026 event (Aivah).

Both readings point the same way: you stop paying only for access to a tool, and start getting finished work.

How is GaaS different from SaaS?

With SaaS, a person logs in, clicks through screens and moves the work along. With GaaS, you describe the job and the limits, and AI does the steps, then hands back a result, or a question when it's unsure.

Industry writers broadly agree on one point: GaaS sits on top of SaaS, it doesn't replace it. Your CRM stays the place records live (Feluda).

Why should a business move from SaaS to GaaS?

Because most of the cost of software is the people time spent operating it. A subscription is easy to see on the bill. The hours spent copying data between tools, writing the same follow-up email or building the same weekly report are harder to see, but they add up.

GaaS goes after those hours:

  • Less copy-paste. AI moves information between the tools you already pay for.
  • Drafts ready before you start. Emails, reports, listings and summaries written from your own data.
  • Answers without digging. Staff ask a question and get the answer with its source.
  • Work keeps moving. Routine steps don't wait for someone to be free.

The point isn't fewer people. It's people doing the parts that need a person.

What kind of work suits GaaS?

A simple test: if the job is repeated often, has clear inputs and a result someone can check in under a minute, it's a good first candidate.

Examples from the sectors we work with:

  • Education: answering course and admissions questions from your own handbook.
  • Nonprofit: first drafts of donor thank-you notes and grant report sections.
  • Real estate: listing descriptions and lead follow-ups after a viewing.
  • Hospitality: replies to common guest questions and review responses for approval.
  • Engineering firms: turning site notes into a report draft.
  • Solid waste management: sorting service requests and summarising complaints by area.

Work that needs fresh judgement every time, like a hiring decision or a legal sign-off, should stay with people. AI can prepare, but a person decides.

What are the risks, and how do you manage them?

AI can be confidently wrong. So a good GaaS setup has guardrails built in:

  • Approvals. Anything that goes out or changes a record can wait for a person's yes.
  • Sources shown. Answers point back to the document they came from.
  • Logs. Every action is recorded, so you can see what happened.
  • Privacy. Your data stays private to your business and access follows your roles.
  • Clear limits. The AI only touches the tools and actions you allow.

How do you move from SaaS to GaaS without breaking things?

Start small. Don't rebuild everything at once.

  1. List the repeat work. Ask your team what they do every week that feels like copying.
  2. Pick one job. Choose the one that's frequent, low-risk and easy to check.
  3. Define "done". Agree what a good result looks like before any AI is built.
  4. Run it with approvals on. People check every result at first.
  5. Loosen as trust grows. Once results are reliably right, let the low-risk parts run on their own.
  6. Add the next job.

Keep your SaaS tools. They stay the records. GaaS is the layer that does the work across them.

Do small businesses need GaaS, or is it only for big companies?

Smaller teams often feel the benefit sooner, because each person already covers several roles. Handing one repeat job to AI can free real time for a team of five. You don't need a data science team; that's what a GaaS provider is for.

FAQ

What does GaaS stand for?

We use GaaS to mean Generative AI as a Service. Many sources also use it for Agentic AI as a Service. Both mean AI that does work for you, rather than software you operate.

Is GaaS replacing SaaS?

No. GaaS works on top of SaaS. Your existing tools stay as the place your data lives.

Is GaaS safe for customer data?

It can be, if it's set up with private data handling, role-based access, approvals and logs.
